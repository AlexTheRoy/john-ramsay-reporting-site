Every populated Washington ZIP code scored for earthquake and wildfire, with inland flooding and landslide as smaller inputs, from the FEMA National Risk Index (December 2025 release) at census-tract resolution, weighted to where each ZIP's residents live, and grouped into four tiers. Earthquake is the hazard most homes west of the Cascades share; wildfire is what sets the east-slope, Okanogan, Spokane-area and Columbia Basin ZIPs apart. Section 04 is the recommended treatment for Home leads, section 05 covers Seattle and King County, and section 06 matches the tiers against the 3,046 GOAL leads delivered from January 1 to October 14, 2026. 03

Scoring system

Public federal data at census-tract resolution

What goes into the score
  • EarthquakeNRI earthquake risk: modeled shaking likelihood times the building value exposed, scored 0 to 100 nationally. Half of Washington housing sits in ZIPs scoring above 91.2, so the cut points are set within Washington. Dense, high-value urban cores score highest.
  • WildfireNRI wildfire risk: modeled burn likelihood and intensity times the value exposed. The tier rules also use the share of a ZIP's residents in tracts FEMA rates Relatively High or Very High for wildfire, which picks out the wildland-urban edge.
  • Smaller inputsNRI inland flooding and landslide risk: added to the composite score at lower weight; shown in the spreadsheet with winter weather.
  • Not scoredHail is not a meaningful Washington peril: half of Washington housing sits in ZIPs scoring below 7.7 nationally for it, and 1.0% of residents live in tracts FEMA rates high for it, so it is left out.
  • AvailabilityCarrier models (year built, foundation, seismic retrofit, defensible space) are property-level and not published by ZIP. This composite uses public federal data on the same hazards.

Tier rules
  • ExtremeEarthquake score 96.5+, or a wildfire score of 90+ with 50%+ of the ZIP's residents in tracts rated Relatively High or Very High for wildfire
  • HighEarthquake score 94.5+, 20%+ of residents in high-wildfire tracts, or composite score 82+
  • ModerateEarthquake score 91+, 5%+ of residents in high-wildfire tracts, or composite score 75+
  • LowEverything else.
  • How Extreme is setExtreme is sized to mark about one Washington home in eight (12.5% of housing). Earthquake 96.5+ alone covers 7.0% of housing; the wildfire arm adds 5.5%, mostly small ZIPs east of the Cascades where most residents live in high-wildfire tracts.

Home leads: recommended treatment

Extreme tier gets an appetite check · High tier gets a construction and deductible review

Check appetite · Extreme · 177 ZIPs · 12.5% of WA housing Construction and deductible review · High · 159 ZIPs · 23.2% No change · 261 ZIPs · 64.3%
Excluding every Extreme ZIP would remove 12.5% of Washington's housing from Home; adding the High tier would bring it to 35.7%. We do not recommend a blanket exclusion. We recommend excluding only the ZIPs where new home business cannot be written today, and treating the rest of the Extreme and High tiers as a conversation: year built and foundation, earthquake coverage and how its deductible works, and wildfire questions where they apply.
Home treatment by tier
  • ExtremeCheck appetite. Confirm the ZIP is open for new home business before the first call. In earthquake ZIPs, ask the year built and whether the house is bolted to its foundation, and walk through earthquake coverage and its deductible. In wildfire ZIPs, ask about defensible space, roof material and road access.
  • HighConstruction and deductible review. Ask the year built and about any seismic retrofit, offer earthquake coverage and explain how its percentage deductible works; in wildfire-edge ZIPs add the roof and defensible-space questions.
  • ModerateNo change. Standard quote; a sentence on earthquake coverage is still worth it west of the Cascades.
  • LowNo change. Standard quote.
Two kinds of Extreme ZIP
  • Earthquake41 ZIPs reach Extreme on earthquake (score 96.5+), holding 231,303 housing units: Seattle & King County 16, South Sound 15, Olympic Peninsula & Coast 9, Puget Sound North 1.
  • Wildfire136 ZIPs reach Extreme on wildfire (wildfire score 90+ with half or more of residents in high-wildfire tracts), holding 181,293 housing units: Central Washington 61, Spokane & Northeast 43, Southwest Washington 16, Tri-Cities & Southeast 15, South Sound 1.
  • Both0 ZIPs meet both rules.

Earthquake and wildfire in the conversation

Questions that make a Washington home quote faster and stickier

Why it matters here
  • Earthquake73% of housing west of the Cascades sits in ZIPs scoring 90+ for earthquake nationally. A standard home policy does not cover earthquake damage; it is added as separate coverage with its own percentage deductible. Offering it on the first call, and explaining the deductible plainly, builds trust and avoids surprises.
  • ConstructionYear built, foundation type and any seismic retrofit (bolting, cripple-wall bracing) are the questions that shape an earthquake quote in older Seattle and Tacoma neighborhoods.
  • WildfireIn wildfire ZIPs, ask about defensible space, roof and siding material, and road access on the first call. Having the answers ready keeps the quote moving and shows the client you know their area.
  • Flooding18.3% of Washingtonians live in tracts FEMA rates high for inland flooding. Flood is also excluded from a standard home policy, so a flood quote is a natural follow-up near rivers.
  • AutoFire, flood and falling-tree damage to a car is paid under comprehensive coverage. On every Auto quote in a High or Extreme ZIP, confirm comprehensive is included; it is also the natural opening to quote the home.

Data & files

  • Risk sourceFEMA National Risk Index, census tract table, December 2025 release (earthquake, wildfire, inland flooding, landslide, winter weather), all 1,772 Washington tracts. The December 2025 release reports river flooding as inland flooding.
  • GeographyU.S. Census Bureau 2020 ZIP Code Tabulation Areas and the 2020 ZCTA-to-tract relationship file. The file reports land-area overlaps, not population, so each tract's values are weighted into a ZIP by the tract's population times the share of its land inside the ZIP. Tract parts outside Washington are not scored. Housing counts from ACS 2020–2024.
  • LeadsAll 3,046 GOAL leads for this account, Jan 1 to Oct 14, 2026 (Home and Home (LM) homeowner leads, Auto and Auto (New)), counted by ZIP. 1,696 of 1,697 Home leads and 1,343 of 1,349 Auto leads matched a populated Washington ZIP; the others used PO-box or non-residential ZIPs (98082, 98160, 98353, 98507, 98666).
  • LimitsA ZIP score describes the ZIP, not a house. Year built, foundation, roof and defensible space decide each quote; the tiers decide where to ask first.
  • FilesJohn-Ramsay-WA-ZIP-Data-10-14-26.csv · every ZIP, every score, tier and treatment column, plus the demographics used in the companion report.
